note=The real-Internet tier: the SAME full multi-edge topology as the other tiers (scenarios/five-edge-100.yaml, 103 services) with the cloud collector running on Crane Cloud (reached over HTTP across the public Internet) and the 5-device/100-service edge half running locally. Each edge device's telemetry to the cloud goes out through its own source IP (10.30.0.<device>, NAT'd to the uplink), so a partition is a REAL per-device cut: an iptables DROP of that device's traffic to the Crane host, while the harness keeps polling Crane over the host's own address. The collector RCA loop ticks every 500 ms, so one tick is the time unit; one sim step maps to one real tick (E3). The edge services report at 200 ms, the same rate as the single-node, k3s and OpenStack tiers: the free tier sustains all 100 services at that rate (the cloud reaches the full 103/103 visible), so the edge time-to-diagnosis is about one tick. Median is 560 ms (ratio 1.12 vs the sim median, within +/-20%) and P95 is 888 ms, compared against the simulation's own P95 of 1000 ms (ratio 0.89, within +/-20%): a percentile is judged against the same percentile, not against the median. One WAN-tier setting remains, disclosed: cuts are capped at 40 s because a longer partition over the uncontrolled Internet does not cleanly re-establish all of a device's HTTP streams on heal, and the harness flushes conntrack and waits for the cloud to recover between events. With this, the edge collector localises its device's fault locally (100%), the Crane cloud is blind during every cut (100%, cannot localise the edge fault), and it loses exactly the cut device's services (cloud_min_visible = 103 minus the device size), on a genuine Internet path.
