## Effect sizes between the two methods

The edge-fault success risk difference is the decentralised edge-fault success rate minus the centralised one, so a positive number means the decentralised method wins. The latency difference is the centralised mean minus the decentralised mean over the runs that localized, with Cohen's d as a standardised size. At the longest partition the centralised method only localizes the cloud-side culprits it can still see, so its latency figure there covers just those easy cases and the latency comparison stops being meaningful. The success collapse is the real story at that point.

## How to read this

At `partition_dur = 0` the two methods are statistically indistinguishable, which is the control. As the partition grows, the centralised collector's mean localization latency climbs, because it has to wait for the link to heal before it can see the edge again, and its edge-fault success collapses to zero once the partition outlasts the observation window. The decentralised method stays flat at roughly one step and near 100% throughout. That gap is the operational sovereignty result. The cross-boundary KB column shows the telemetry the centralised design has to ship off the edge, which is a data residency and bandwidth cost the decentralised design avoids.

The simulation is seeded and deterministic, so there is no warm-up phase to discard and the same command reproduces the same numbers. The only randomness is the seeded fault placement and measurement noise, both documented in `crates/sim/src/main.rs`.
